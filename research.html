People, Organisations & Communities — Four Intersecting Research Clusters

Research Philosophy

What Drives My Research

"I believe research should begin with questions that matter to people, organisations and society. My research is driven by an interest in understanding how workplaces, technology, organisational practices and human behaviour influence individual and organisational outcomes. I find myself particularly drawn to questions around employability, skill development, psychological safety, inclusive workplaces, people analytics and the changing nature of work in the age of artificial intelligence. I aim to produce research that goes beyond academic publication and contributes to practical improvements in organisational decision-making, employee well-being, educational practices and career readiness. Ultimately, I want my research to bridge the gap between theory and practice and create knowledge that is meaningful, relevant and capable of making a positive difference in people's lives."

Doctoral Research

PhD Thesis

Completed Doctoral Research · 2022

Employability Skills of Management Graduates — Perceptions of Employers, Educators, and MBA Students in Selected Districts of Andhra Pradesh, India
UniversityAcharya Nagarjuna University, Guntur
DisciplineCommerce & Management
Year of Award2022
MethodologyMixed Methods — Survey & Qualitative

This research examined the employability skills of management graduates by triangulating the perspectives of three distinct stakeholder groups — employers hiring MBA graduates, educators delivering management programmes, and students themselves. The study was conducted across selected districts of Andhra Pradesh and explored the gaps between skill expectations, skill delivery and self-perceived skill readiness. The findings have contributed to ongoing conversations about curriculum reform, graduate attributes and the design of management education in India.
